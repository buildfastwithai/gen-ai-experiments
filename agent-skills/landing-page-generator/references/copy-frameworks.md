# Copy Frameworks and CTA Strategy

## Framework Selection

| Framework | Best When | Audience Awareness Level |
|-----------|-----------|--------------------------|
| PAS (Problem > Agitate > Solve) | Audience knows the problem | Problem-aware |
| AIDA (Attention > Interest > Desire > Action) | Audience needs education | Unaware to problem-aware |
| BAB (Before > After > Bridge) | Audience wants transformation | Solution-aware |

Rule of thumb: cold ad traffic is usually problem-aware at best (PAS or AIDA); email lists and retargeting are solution-aware (BAB).

## PAS Applied to the Page

| Section | PAS Element | Copy Approach |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Hero headline | Problem | Name the pain directly |
| Problem section | Agitate | Show consequences of inaction, quantify the cost |
| Solution section | Solve | Introduce product as the answer |
| CTA | Solve | Clear action to access the solution |

**Example:**
- Hero: "Your team wastes 4 hours every week on manual reporting"
- Problem: "That is 200 hours a year — the equivalent of losing a full-time employee to spreadsheets. Meanwhile, your competitors are shipping features."
- Solution: "[Product] automates your reporting. Set it once, get reports every Monday morning."
- CTA: "Automate my reports — start free trial"

## AIDA Applied to the Page

| Section | AIDA Element | Copy Approach |
|---------|--------------|---------------|
| Hero headline | Attention | Bold, specific claim or surprising stat |
| Benefits section | Interest | Expand with relevant details and benefits |
| Social proof | Desire | Show proof, paint the transformation |
| CTA | Action | Clear, compelling call to action |

## BAB Applied to the Page

| Section | BAB Element | Copy Approach |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Hero + Problem | Before | Current painful state |
| Solution + Benefits | After | Desired future state, concrete and vivid |
| How it works | Bridge | How the product gets them there in 3 steps |

## Hero Copy Rules

- Headline: 6-12 words, contains the primary benefit
- Subheadline: 1-2 sentences, adds specificity or the "how"
- Message match: the headline must mirror the ad/email that drove the click — mismatched expectations are the top bounce cause

| Traffic Source | Headline Approach |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Paid search | Lead with the searched keyword |
| Paid social | Lead with the ad's hook/benefit |
| Email campaign | Lead with the email's promise |
| Organic search | Lead with the comprehensive answer |
| Referral/partner | Lead with the referrer's context |

## CTA Copy

Formula: `[Action Verb] + [What They Get]`

- "Start my free trial" — strong verb, ownership language, names the outcome
- "Get the complete guide"
- "See pricing for my team"
- Avoid weak verbs as the first word: submit, click, send, go, enter, continue

**Placement** (all CTAs drive the same single action):

| Position | Purpose | Copy Angle |
|----------|---------|------------|
| Hero | Primary conversion point | Action + benefit |
| After how-it-works/benefits | Capture momentum | Reinforce ("See it in action") |
| Final CTA section | Last chance | Urgency or social ("Join 2,800+ teams") |

**Friction reducers** under every CTA button: "No credit card required", "Set up in 2 minutes", "Cancel anytime", "Free for 14 days". Pick the 1-2 that are true for this offer — never invent claims.

## Voice Rules

- Write in the reader's language, not the product's ("stop chasing invoices" not "streamline AR workflows")
- Benefits over features: every feature line answers "so what?"
- Specific beats superlative: "reduces churn 23%" beats "best-in-class retention"
- Use "you" more than the product name
- Cut every word that doesn't earn its place — landing page copy is scanned, not read
