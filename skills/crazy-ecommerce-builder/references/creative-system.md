# Creative system

Use this reference to turn product details into an art direction that is strange, coherent, and commercially useful.

## Concept formula

Build the direction from:

`material or product truth + customer emotion + cultural visual language + commerce constraint`

Example:

- product truth: pottery and glass are fused into one cup;
- emotion: discovery with a small dose of danger;
- visual language: Swiss-punk product catalog and hard-flash studio photography;
- commerce constraint: the hybrid seam and price must be clear immediately;
- thesis: **Half earth. Half ghost.** Use the seam as the site-wide signature.

Avoid starting with adjectives such as “modern,” “premium,” or “bold.” Start with the product's mechanism, ritual, material, origin, audience behavior, or contradiction.

## Product-tension prompts

Find one tension that is true:

- ancient × futuristic;
- fragile × protective;
- serious performance × childish joy;
- natural material × synthetic color;
- microscopic detail × monumental scale;
- clinical precision × handmade irregularity;
- everyday ritual × ceremonial drama;
- local origin × speculative future;
- quiet function × loud identity.

Invent another tension when these do not fit. Do not force a preset onto the company.

## Direction families

Use these as starting points, never as finished templates.

### Specimen laboratory

Best for material innovation, formulation, skincare, food science, technical apparel, and engineered objects. Use specimen labels, measurement marks, macro textures, transparent layers, cool light, and evidence-led copy. Keep essential buying details warmer and simpler than the art direction.

### Underground editorial

Best for culture-first products, fashion, coffee, music, collectible objects, and challenger brands. Use aggressive type scale, hard flash, imperfect alignment, editorial captions, limited colors, and sharp copy. Avoid illegible type and fake rebellion.

### Ritual surrealism

Best for fragrance, food, wellness, homeware, and products with strong routines. Use repeated gestures, impossible scale, dreamlike sets, ceremonial pacing, and sensory copy. Keep the product depiction accurate.

### Industrial theater

Best for tools, furniture, equipment, mobility, audio, and durable goods. Use exploded views, shipping marks, warning colors, structural grids, raw material details, and large physical shadows. Do not invent performance specifications.

### Pop artifact

Best for snacks, toys, accessories, cosmetics, and joyful consumer goods. Use saturated fields, sculptural product stacks, sticker-like facts, playful naming, and crisp direct lighting. Keep the palette disciplined: one dominant, one disruptive, one neutral.

### Quiet anomaly

Best for premium audiences who reject loud design. Use extreme whitespace, one surreal physical detail, unusually slow pacing, restrained typography, and macro material imagery. “Crazy” can be the single impossible thing, not constant noise.

## Visual-world specification

Write these choices before building:

- **Palette:** dominant, disruptive, neutral, and text colors with roles.
- **Typography:** display behavior, body behavior, numeric/product-label behavior.
- **Composition:** grid rule and the deliberate way it breaks.
- **Photography:** backdrop, lens/framing, lighting, shadow, material rendering.
- **Texture:** none, paper grain, raw material, scan noise, halftone, or another justified surface.
- **Motion:** one continuous behavior and one interaction response.
- **Signature device:** a motif derived from the product truth.
- **Copy voice:** three traits and two phrases the brand would never say.

## Coherence test

Before implementation, confirm:

1. Can the direction be explained without naming a UI trend?
2. Does the signature device come from the product?
3. Can a customer still identify the product, price position, and next action?
4. Will product cards and the hero feel like one campaign?
5. Is at least one design choice ownable by this company?

If not, revise the thesis rather than adding more decoration.
